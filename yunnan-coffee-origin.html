The origin

China’s coffee heartland is more varied than a single number can show.

Yunnan grows almost all of China’s coffee. Yet its most interesting story is not simply how much it produces. It is how producers across a vast mountain province are learning to express difference.

At the end of 2024, official figures recorded more than 1.2 million mu of coffee land—about 80,000 hectares—and annual output above 140,000 tonnes. Both represented more than 98% of China’s total coffee area and production.

From volume to identity

For much of its modern history, Yunnan was understood as a dependable commercial origin. That description reflected the market, but it never described every farm or every possibility. Higher elevation plantings, more selective harvesting, renewed interest in varieties such as Typica and careful experiments in washed, natural, honey and controlled-fermentation processing are changing how the region is tasted.

What emerges is not one fixed “Yunnan flavour.” We often find a recognisable sweetness, but also florals, citrus, tropical fruit, warm spice and tea-like clarity. Variety, elevation, shade, soil, harvest timing and processing all change the cup. The value of the origin lies in this diversity—not in making every producer conform to one profile.

Scale introduces Yunnan. Individual farms, choices and landscapes reveal its character.

One province, many coffee landscapes

Yunnan’s coffee regions are separated by mountains, rivers and local climates. Each asks producers to read the land differently.

Baoshan

One of Yunnan’s historic coffee areas, known for balanced cups, rounded sweetness and a long relationship with small-bean Arabica.

Pu’er

A major centre of production where altitude and varied processing can create rich, full-bodied coffees with expanding specialty potential.

Dehong

A warm western border region whose coffees can show fruit-forward and floral qualities when cultivation and selection are carefully managed.

Menglian

A southern landscape where committed estates are developing clean, sweet coffees through close field management and thoughtful processing.

Quality must survive the journey

Specialty quality can be created at the farm and still be lost in transit. For YD Selects, origin work continues after the coffee leaves the mill: stable storage in China, careful loading, suitable transport, arrival checks and quality control in the United Kingdom are all part of protecting the producer’s work.

Our role is to connect the detail of each lot with roasters who want to understand it. That means sharing context, not simply a score; building demand that rewards progress; and returning knowledge to producers so the next harvest can move forward.

A good origin keeps becoming

We do not believe Yunnan needs to imitate another coffee country to earn international recognition. Its future lies in making its own differences more legible: supporting producers, developing repeatable quality and giving each region and estate room to speak in its own voice.

That is the Yunnan we select from—a large origin, seen closely.
